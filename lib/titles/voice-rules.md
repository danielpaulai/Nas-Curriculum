# Voice Rules — Nas.com School (formerly AI Business School)

Owners Nuseir Yassin (CEO) and Alex Dweck (CEO/COO) reject AI-sounding copy on sight. These rules are extracted from analyzing 432 past session titles, the de-AI-ify principles, and the May 2026 strategic pivot meetings.

## STRATEGIC POSITIONING (post-May-2026 rebrand)

The school is being rebranded from **AI Business School** → **Nas.com School** (positioned as the marketing school for Nas.com store owners). Internalize:

- **Audience**: paid Nas.com subscribers running e-commerce stores. They already have a store. They need marketing.
- **Content ratio**: ~80% marketing-anchored, ~20% pure tools/AI tactics.
- **Topic frame**: Never "what is X" (definitional). Always "how to market X" or "how to sell more X using AI."
- **Realistic > aspirational**: "Make your first $1,000" not "$1M offer." Nisara (founder) reads aspirational claims as scammy and will kill them.
- **"Sales" is a trigger word**: it depresses RSVP unless the trainer is Danny or Marco. Prefer "Market", "Find Customers", "Drive Traffic", "Convert Visitors", "Get Orders".

## Banned Words (hard fail)

### Verbs that scream AI
- unlock, unleash, harness, leverage, embark, dive (into), delve
- empower, navigate, foster, cultivate, elevate
- revolutionize, transform (as a verb describing experiences)
- streamline, optimize, synergize, supercharge (overused)

### Adjectives that scream AI
- cutting-edge, state-of-the-art, next-gen, next-level
- revolutionary, transformative, game-changing, game-changer
- unprecedented, paradigm-shifting
- comprehensive, holistic, seamless, robust
- innovative, groundbreaking, pioneering
- ever-evolving, dynamic, multifaceted

### Phrases that scream AI
- "in today's fast-paced world"
- "in the age of AI"
- "the world of [X]"
- "take your [X] to the next level"
- "boost your [X]"
- "navigate the complexities of"
- "demystify"
- "unleash the power of"
- "the ultimate guide to"
- "everything you need to know about"
- "a beginner's guide to"
- "mastering the art of"

### Filler that adds no value
- "with ease", "effortlessly", "with confidence"
- "in just a few clicks", "with a single click"
- "the future of", "the new era of"

## Banned Title Structures

❌ "The X Guide to Y"
❌ "5 Tips for Mastering X" (Nas-style is "5 [Specific Outcomes]" not tips-for-mastering)
❌ "Why You Should Care About X"
❌ "X 101"
❌ "Introduction to X"
❌ "Everything About X"
❌ "Beginner's Guide to X"
❌ "What is [Print-on-Demand / Dropshipping / TikTok Shop / E-commerce]?" — definitional / educational framing. We don't teach what things are; we teach how to MARKET them.

## Aspirational claims (banned post-May-2026)

❌ "$1M offer" / "Million-dollar business" / "7-figure store"
❌ "100K offer" / "Six-figure income"
❌ "Build a Million-Dollar X" (unless evidenced by a specific student case)

Use realistic, attainable milestones instead:
✅ "Make your first $1,000 this month"
✅ "Get your first 10 sales this week"
✅ "Land your first 100 customers"
✅ "Earn $1,000 in recurring revenue"

## The "Sales" word rule

The word **"sales"** depresses RSVP. Data: every session with "sales" in the title underperforms unless Danny or Marco is the trainer (familiar faces who derisk it for the audience).

Replace "sales" with:
- "Market" / "Marketing"
- "Convert visitors" / "Convert browsers"
- "Get orders" / "Drive orders"
- "Find customers" / "Land customers"
- "Drive traffic"
- "Earn from your store"

✅ OK: "Sell" as an action verb in some contexts ("Sell Your First Course in 48 Hours") — but use sparingly, never more than once per batch of 5.
❌ Avoid: "Sales funnel", "Sales agent" (in title — fine in description), "Sales strategy", "Drive sales", "Boost sales".

## The 5-Second Test

Read the title aloud. If it sounds like a LinkedIn AI bot wrote it → kill it. If it sounds like Nuseir saying it on stage → ship it.

## The Marketing School Test (post-May-2026)

For every batch, ask:
1. Would a Nas.com store owner who already has a store click on this?
2. Does the title promise marketing help, not just AI exposure?
3. Is the outcome realistic enough that Nisara won't read it as scammy?
4. Does it name a concrete deliverable (asset, calendar, channel, customer count)?

If all four are yes → ship. If any is no → rewrite.
